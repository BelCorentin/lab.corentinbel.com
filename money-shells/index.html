a short voyage through money

From cowries to crypto

I collect seashells. One small, glossy one, the money cowry, paid for things across three continents for centuries. That got me wondering what else people have agreed to treat as money, and why it worked.

Ten stops, roughly in time order. Scroll to travel, click any object for its story, and at the end, judge them yourself.

scroll down

contestedDid barter come first?

The textbook story, going back to Adam Smith, runs: people bartered, barter was clumsy, so they picked a commodity as money, and credit came later. Anthropologists have looked hard for the barter economy at the start of that story and not found one. Caroline Humphrey wrote in 1985 that no example of a barter economy, pure and simple, has ever been described.

David Graeber (2011) went further: credit and debt came first, written in temple accounts long before coins. Many historians accept the critique of the barter myth; how far his own story holds is still argued. Here, treat it as a lens, not a verdict.

So, what is money?

Economists usually say money does three jobs. Few things on this voyage do all three well. Pick a stop, and rate it from 0 to 5 on each job. The dashed shape is my rough take; the filled one is yours. Argue with me.

Medium of exchangeYou can hand it over and people take it, without a second thought.
Unit of accountPrices, debts and wages are written in it. It is the ruler you measure value with.
Store of valuePut it away for a year and it still buys about as much.

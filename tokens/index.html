what does a prompt cost the planet?

every token is a few joules on a GPU somewhere. add the chips it runs on, the months of training before it, the cooling around it — then multiply by how much you actually use it. here's an honest order-of-magnitude calculator, from sand to sentence.

zoom out: the whole industry

all data centers (not just AI) used ~415 TWh in 2024, about 1.5 % of world electricity, and the IEA projects roughly a doubling by 2030 — AI is the main driver. here is how that stacks against other things, in Mt CO₂ per year.

what to take home

  • one chat message is tiny — about the energy of a few seconds of a microwave. guilt over single prompts is misplaced.
  • agents are not chat. an agent re-reads a 50–150k-token context on every tool call, hundreds of times a day. that's 1,000–10,000× a casual user.
  • the grid beats the model. where the GPU sits changes the answer more than which model you pick.
  • training is big, but shared. a frontier run is tens of thousands of tonnes, yet split over trillions of tokens it's often a minority of your slice.
  • diet and flights still dominate. even heavy agent use lands well below one transatlantic flight or a year of eating beef weekly.
  • everything here is ±3×. labs don't publish per-token energy. treat numbers as orders of magnitude, not invoices.

per-token energyclass defaults (IT energy per 1k output tokens): small 0.12 Wh, mid 0.5 Wh, frontier 1.5 Wh — chosen so a ~500-token mid-size reply ≈0.3 Wh with overhead, matching Epoch / Google / OpenAI. input tokens weighted 0.25× an output token, cached 0.025× (roughly API price ratios, which track compute; Epoch's long-input figures suggest input could be heavier). thinking tokens count as output.

Luccioni et al. 2023, BLOOM LCA: embodied = 22 % and idle = 29 % of 50.5 t total. here: ~400 kg per GPU slot incl. host + building share, 5-year life at 60 % use → ≈15 g per kWh of IT energy.

fungal facts

a few things that hold up under a closer look

🌲

the wood wide web is real

tree roots and fungal mycelium form mycorrhizal networks that link separate trees underground — nutrients and chemical signals measurably move between them. how much of that is cooperation versus each fungus quietly serving itself is still an active, contested question.

🍄

the biggest known organism is a fungus

a single Armillaria ostoyae individual in Oregon's Malheur forest spans roughly 2,400 acres of mostly-underground mycelium — one connected organism, plausibly thousands of years old.

🧵

a pinch of soil, kilometres of thread

hyphae are only a few micrometres wide, but they pack density: a single gram of healthy soil can contain on the order of a hundred metres of fungal thread, coiled invisibly underfoot.

⚡

mycelium fires like a nervous system

researchers have recorded spontaneous electrical spikes travelling through fungal networks — not thought, but a real spiking pattern that some labs are trying to use as a slow, living logic gate.

🐜

some fungi puppet their host

Ophiocordyceps infects an ant, steers it to climb and clamp onto a leaf at just the right height and humidity, then grows a fruiting body out of its head. the behavioural hijack is precise enough to look scripted.

🧬

fungi are closer to you than to a plant

fungi and animals share a more recent common ancestor than fungi and plants do — both branch off the same "opisthokont" side of the eukaryote tree. mushrooms are, distantly, closer kin to us.

📦

you can grow packaging instead of moulding it

mycelium fed on agricultural waste binds into a dense foam as it grows — already used as compostable packaging, leather-like sheets and building insulation, no petroleum required.

🔁

cut it up, it doesn't die

a mycelium colony has no fixed body plan — a single fragment can regrow the network from scratch. it behaves less like an animal and more like a distributed, self-healing map.
